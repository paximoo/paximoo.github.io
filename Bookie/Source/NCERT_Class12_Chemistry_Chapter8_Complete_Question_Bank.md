### AUDIT OF REMAINING FACTS (Bonus One-Liners)

*The following minor details from Page 1 to Section 8.2 were extracted during self-audit to guarantee 100% coverage:*

1. **Precious vs. Industrial Metals:** NCERT explicitly classifies Silver (Ag), Gold (Au), and Platinum (Pt) as *precious metals*, while Iron (Fe), Copper (Cu), and Titanium (Ti) are explicitly labeled as *industrially important metals*.
2. **Lanthanoids and Actinoids Nomenclature:** The 4f and 5f inner transition series are formally named *lanthanoids* and *actinoids*, respectively.
3. **Flanking Blocks:** The d-block is described as occupying the "large middle section flanked by s- and p-blocks in the periodic table".
4. **Group 3 Formal Inclusion:** The f-block elements are formal members of Group 3 of the periodic table.
5. **Element 112 Symbol in Table 8.1:** In Table 8.1 of the old NCERT edition, element Z = 112 is denoted by the official systematic symbol **Uub** (Ununbium, outer configuration 6d¹⁰ 7s²).
6. **Characteristic Properties List:** NCERT lists five key characteristic properties resulting from partly filled d-orbitals: display of a variety of oxidation states, formation of colored ions, complex formation with ligands, catalytic property, and paramagnetic behavior.

*The following fine factual details from Sections 8.3.3 to 8.3.5 were extracted during self-audit to guarantee 100% NCERT line-by-line coverage:*

1. **Reorganisation & Exchange Energy in Unipositive Ions:** The removal of one electron from a neutral 3d atom alters the relative energies of 4s and 3d orbitals such that unipositive ions have dⁿ configurations with no 4s electrons; this process is accompanied by reorganisation energy and gains in exchange energy as electrons transfer from s into d orbitals.
2. **Third Ionisation Enthalpy Break:** There is a marked break between the third ionisation enthalpies of Mn²⁺ (3260 kJ mol⁻¹) and Fe²⁺ (2962 kJ mol⁻¹), reflecting the loss of extra exchange stability when removing an electron from the 3d⁵ subshell of Mn²⁺.

4. **Species up to Manganese with Maximum Oxidation State:** The species exhibiting maximum stable oxidation states corresponding to the sum of s and d electrons up to Mn are TiO₂ (or TiO²⁺), VO₂⁺, Cr₂O₇²⁻, and MnO₄⁻.
5. **Abrupt Stability Drop post-Manganese:** Beyond Manganese, higher oxidation states decrease abruptly in stability, giving typical stable species Fe(II,III), Co(II,III), Ni(II), Cu(I,II), and Zn(II).
