**Q10.**

* **Assertion:** Iron pyrites (FeS₂) is usually not preferred for the industrial extraction of iron.
* **Reason:** Roasting of iron pyrites produces SO₂ gas, which causes environmental pollution.

**Q11.**

* **Assertion:** Magnetic separation can be employed to concentrate haematite and magnetite ores.
* **Reason:** Magnetic separation is possible whenever either the ore or the gangue component is capable of being attracted by a magnetic field.

**Q12.**

* **Assertion:** Froth floatation method enabled the profitable extraction of copper even from low-grade copper ores.
* **Reason:** Froth floatation relies on differential wetting properties, where copper-bearing sulphide particles are selectively caught in soapsuds/froth and carried to the top.

**Q13.**

* **Assertion:** In the concentration of an ore containing both ZnS and PbS, NaCN is added as a depressant.
* **Reason:** NaCN forms a soluble complex with ZnS on the surface, preventing ZnS from coming into the froth, while PbS floats with the froth.

**Q14.**

* **Assertion:** Digestion of bauxite ore with NaOH solution is carried out at elevated temperatures (473–523 K) and high pressures (35–36 bar).
* **Reason:** Al₂O₃ is amphoteric and selectively leaches out as soluble sodium aluminate \[Na\[Al(OH)₄\]\] under high temperature and pressure, leaving behind insoluble Fe₂O₃ and TiO₂ impurities.

**Q15.**

* **Assertion:** Leaching of gold and silver ores using dilute NaCN or KCN solution requires continuous passage of air/oxygen.
* **Reason:** Oxygen acts as an oxidising agent to oxidise elemental gold/silver M(0) to M(+1) state in the soluble cyano complex \[M(CN)₂\]⁻.

**Q16.**

* **Assertion:** In the recovery of silver or gold from their soluble cyano complexes, metallic zinc is added to the solution.
* **Reason:** Zinc acts as a reducing agent and displaces silver/gold from \[M(CN)₂\]⁻ to form the complex \[Zn(CN)₄\]²⁻.

## AUDIT OF REMAINING FACTS

*(Minor factual details from Page 1 to Section 6.2.4 converted into Bonus One-Liners)*

1. **Pre-concentration processing:** Before proceeding for concentration, ores are graded and crushed to reasonable size.
2. **General principles of metallurgy:** Thermodynamics illustrates why only a certain reducing element and a minimum specific temperature are suitable for reduction of a metal oxide to the metal in an extraction.
3. **Factors determining concentration steps:** Selection of concentration steps depends upon differences in physical properties of the compound of the metal present and that of the gangue, type of metal, available facilities, and environmental factors.
4. **Hydraulic washing mechanism:** Uses an upward stream of running water to wash powdered ore; lighter gangue particles are washed away and heavier ores are left behind.
5. **Magnetic separation apparatus details:** Ground ore is carried on a conveyer belt which passes over a magnetic roller (schematic shown in Fig 6.1).
6. **Froth removal mechanism:** The froth formed in froth floatation carrying mineral particles is light and is skimmed off, then dried for recovery of ore particles.
7. **Bauxite leaching byproduct:** CO₂ neutralisation of sodium aluminate produces sodium hydrogen carbonate (NaHCO₃) as a soluble byproduct.

## AUDIT OF REMAINING FACTS

*(Minor factual details from Section 6.3 to 6.4.0 converted into Bonus One-Liners)*

1. **Reason for oxide conversion:** Sulphide ores are converted to oxides prior to reduction because oxides are easier to reduce than sulphides.
2. **Volatile matter escape:** Calcination involves heating the ore such that volatile matter escapes, leaving behind a porous metal oxide.
3. **Reverberatory furnace charge:** Ground ore mixed with silica is fed through the charge hopper into the reverberatory furnace.
4. **Reverberatory furnace lining:** Modern reverberatory furnaces feature magnesite and silica refractories with tie rods and hangers.
5. **Sulfuric acid byproduct:** The SO₂ gas generated during sulphide ore roasting is captured and industrially converted to H₂SO₄.
6. **Definition of Flux & Slag:** Flux is added during smelting to combine with gangue to form slag, which has a lower melting point and separates easily from molten ore/metal.
7. **Ellingham diagram author:** The graphical representation of standard Gibbs energy vs temperature for oxide formation was first introduced by H.J.T. Ellingham.
8. **Plot equation for oxide formation:** Standard Ellingham plots are drawn for the oxidation reaction consuming 1 mole of O₂ gas: 2xM(s) + O₂(g) → 2MₓO(s).
9. **Horizontal plot in Ellingham Diagram:** The curve for C(s) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g) is virtually horizontal because 1 mole of gas forms 1 mole of gas (ΔS ≈ 0).
10. **Other Ellingham Diagrams:** Ellingham diagrams can also be constructed for sulphides and halides, showing why thermal reduction of MₓS is difficult without prior roasting.
